The Forgetting Curve

In 1885 Hermann Ebbinghaus published a series of experiments on his own memory. He memorized lists of nonsense syllables and tested how many he could recall after different delays.

The result was a curve: memory drops steeply in the first hours, then levels off. Most of what we forget, we forget quickly.

Spaced repetition takes advantage of this. Instead of reviewing everything every day, you review an item just as you're about to forget it. Each review pushes the next one further out: one day, three days, a week, a month.

The testing effect adds to it. Trying to recall something strengthens memory more than reading it again. Flashcards work because they make you answer before you see the answer.

None of this needs software, but software makes the scheduling easy. The hard part is deciding what's worth remembering at all.
